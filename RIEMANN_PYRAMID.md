# The Riemann Proof as a Pyramid

Nov 21, 2026 · @Dennis

Levent Alpöge's Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis can be read as eight block statements instead of 16.6 million lines (the Lean files in the copy read here; public reports give about 17.4 million for the full archive). Each block is a proven Lean theorem, and every block’s route to RH has been traced through explicit calls in proof bodies, at the source level (level 8’s internals excepted).

This is Dennis's Pāṇini idea applied to the proof. Pāṇini's rules stand for groups of sounds; each block here stands for a group of proof steps. The Lean proof stays underneath, untouched. A reader accepts each block's conclusion and opens the files only to inspect how it holds.

**What is being summarized.** The archive at `github.com/Deicyde/riemann-hypothesis` has 164,464 Lean files and 16,588,698 lines (counted from a clone on 21 Nov 2026). The Lean and Mathlib communities certified on 21 Nov 2026 that it proves Mathlib's exact `RiemannHypothesis`. Andrew Sutherland's sandboxed rebuild matched the published proof export byte for byte; his two single-threaded kernel runs were still pending at last check.

**Status labels used below.**

- **Proven, traced**: the Lean statement was read, and the call from the level above was found in a proof body.
- **Proven**: the Lean statement was read; its call from above was not traced.
- **Not traced**: named in the proof, but its own proof was not opened.

All Lean names and file paths were checked against the source. The Lean build was not rerun here.

## The pyramid at a glance

```text
1   RH  <=  no zeros right of 1/2
2   no zeros right of 1/2, every Dirichlet character
3   row separation: the functionals tell nonzero rows apart
4   ascent: a nonzero start gives a nonzero endpoint across the chain
5   divisibility: at least r_w*m powers of the prime at each eligible place
6   feasibility: one multiplier works for every vector at each eligible place
6.5 per-translate comparison (ErrX5at, ch_top)
7   chain length: two error terms balance at m ~ sqrt(x)
8   base: theta lift -> Galois representations -> the Row result
```

Read top down: each arrow points from a block to a result its proof uses. Levels 5, 6 and 7 are proven, and their routes to the spine are now traced at the source level (see Open links); the compiled build was not replayed.

## Level by level

Read top down: each level's conclusion is what the level above consumes.

**1. RH from "no zeros right of ½".** Status: proven, traced.

- Lean route used by Main: `rhOfAll := Formalism.VW.rh_of_every allChi`, which calls `Formalism.VW.rh_of_right` (in `RhOfRight.lean`) with ζ(s) ≠ 0 for Re s > ½, s ≠ 1 (the trivial character of modulus 1, with its exception at s = 1). `UpperTheory.riemannHypothesis_of_everyCharacter` is a parallel wrapper with the same argument.
- About 20 lines. Take the trivial character. A zero right of ½ is excluded directly. A zero left of ½ has a mirror zero right of ½ under s ↔ 1−s, so it is excluded too. Only the line remains.
- Called from `Main.lean`: `riemannHypothesis := rhOfAll`, and `rhOfAll := Formalism.VW.rh_of_every allChi`.

**2. No zeros right of ½, for every Dirichlet character.** Status: proven, traced.

- Lean: `UpperTheory.LiteratureTheorems.every_character_of_literature_of_vWin`, which calls `every_character_of_theta_integrals_local_matching`.
- Inputs: a bundle `O` of published results, theta integrals agree, local matching, a covolume estimate, a local Langlands input, and the window hypothesis `hV`.
- `MainConclusion.lean` builds `O` as `⟨h, recTExists, h⟩` and `hV` as `vwin_of_row h`, where `h` is one result, `galoisLocalGlobalStrong_recParameter` (level 8).

**3. Row separation: the functionals tell nonzero rows apart.** Status: proven, traced.

- Lean: `functionalsSeparate_of_howeUnram_of_siegelWeil`, whose whole proof is `real_functionals_separate_of_hypothesis`.
- Called by `nonempty_hyps_definite_side_of_closed_bound`, which `every_character_of_theta_integrals_local_matching` calls.

**4. Ascent: a nonzero start gives a nonzero endpoint across the whole chain.** Status: proven, traced.

- Lean: `Ascent` (in `OffQOfSizeRows.lean`). For a chain Ψ₀ … Ψₘ, if the starting period `per0` is nonzero, some ρ, Φ make the Bessel expression at Ψₘ nonzero.
- Built by `besselPeriod_ascent_of_tower_rungs : TowerEx → Rung0 → RungB → RungJ → Ascent` from four rungs: the tower, the bottom rung, Fourier–Jacobi → Bessel, and Bessel → Fourier–Jacobi.
- Passed by name into `rowSeparation_by_realFunctionals_of_chain_steps` inside `real_functionals_separate_of_hypothesis` (level 3).

**6. Feasibility: at each eligible place, one multiplier works for every vector.** Status: proven, source-traced (NP\_full via ledOK to the spine).

- Lean: `NP_full`. At a fixed place, some nonzero n sends every vector of `MramL` into `NP`; the multiplier is p^(A+B), and A and B change from place to place.
- Uses only the lattice comparison, not the Bessel identity `PerLaw`. It is the gluing hypothesis `hS` that level 5 needs.

So in rank two any tail with j = o(d) keeps the leading term. For the actual family, a one-sided per-box bound Δ ≥ −C·log k along a valid path would cost at most C·J·log k, a fraction O(x⁻⁹) of the leading term; at k = 3 the most negative observed increment is −0.364, against log 3 ≈ 1.10. The sharp rank-two loss constant is 1/2 (Astra: entropy concavity, averaged over a uniform probability; checked, min Δ = −0.4995 at n = 2999). Astra also derived an exact block formula for every (d,2) shape in any rank k, det G\_dual/Q^r = (T−2)^(s−1)(2T−2)∏aᵢ/4 · ∏ over i\<j of 1/(aᵢaⱼ) · ∏ over aᵢ≥2 of 2/(aᵢ(aᵢ−1)), including the integral factor 1/4; it matches dual.py exactly on 2,612 blocks (1,738 of rank above one, k ≤ 5), which proves the prime cutoff for these shapes. Shape-resolved increments (Dennis's Durfee-style reading: record where each box attaches) show the loss depends on placement, and the rank-two 1/2 does not carry over: a box under a long row behaves like rank two (losses −0.43, −0.49, approaching −1/2), while a box that breaks two level rows loses most ((2,2)→(3,2): −0.731 at k = 3, d = 16; (1,1,1)→(2,1,1): −0.664 at k = 4, d = 12). Completing a column or a level pair gains (up to +0.88). All observed losses are below 0.75, under log k. Full-height levels (Astra): for (d,j,…,j) → (d,j+1,j,…,j) in rank k, peeling the j full-height columns (a determinant twist) gives Δ = Δ\_one(d−j, k) + log((j+k−1)/((k−1)(j+1))), checked against dual.py to 10 digits in 4 further cases including (4,6,2). That is the open geometry, and it is the bulk of the tail. Matches dual.py on 15 shapes (k = 3–5) to 10⁻¹⁴. Column steps at k = 4 turn from gain to loss at d = 89 (+0.000163 at d = 88, −0.000167 at d = 89; settling near −0.028 by d = 2000), so completing a column does not always repay. But the total change from one row to the whole block (d, q, …, q, 0) at d = 2000 is a gain in every tested case except k = 4, q = 1 (−0.028): per box +0.02 to +0.05 at k = 4, +0.09 to +0.85 at k = 6 and 8, all below log k. So in family-like shapes the tail does not lower D, and the mean-side bound stays sharp. The hook factor c\_λ is exact, not a guess: it follows from hookNorm, proved in the repo (PolarisationOperatorApplyVariable.lean line 816). Uneven edges (Astra, replayed): raising half the middle rows by one box can give back much of the rectangle’s gain (k = 5, d = 2000, q = 1: +0.831 to +0.198) but the tail still raises D in every sampled case. ## The candidate new mathematics, as one chain

The likely new idea is Alpöge's integer substitute for Stepanov's multiplicity: m theta lifts give a gain linear in m, paid for by error terms that also grow with m, and the two balance at m ≈ √x. Each step below is a Lean statement read in the source; the bracket gives the matching item in Alpöge's timeline.

1. **Multiplicity from the chain** \[item 8\]. `EComp5`: |Ee − m·sumA·lg| ≤ cE·m·B0. The term Ee is m times a fixed amount, up to an error of size m·B0.
2. **Divisibility of the m-fold construction** \[item 10\]. `NuServed`: at least r\_w·m powers of the prime at each eligible place, set locally and glued into one lattice (levels 5 and 6).
3. **Slope step** \[item 13, Bost\]. `muMax_le` turns each certified power into −2·lwP(w) on the maximal slope. `SlopesN_at` then pins the slope in a window: both edges share the gain G = Ga + Fo − Ee, and the per-place divisibility credit is Σ\_j gain\_{j,w} − τ\_w, which vanishes when the certified powers equal the per-lift contributions.
4. **Slope inequality**. `SlopeIneq`: s − log|W| − (1−δ)⁻¹·(4u·log(1 + H/u) + (η + 4√e)·H) ≤ μ. Where the logarithm comes from: in `TheoremFSharp.lean` (folder `HarderNarasimhan`), the proof counts lattice slopes below a moving depth t, bounds the lost rank at depth t by (1−δ)⁻¹·(η + 4·u/(u + t) + 4√e), and integrates with `layer_cake_formula`. `frameB_integral` gives ∫₀ᴴ 4u/(u + t) dt = 4u·log(1 + H/u), and the constant part gives (η + 4√e)·H. The proof then bounds the u·log(1 + H/u) term with `phi_bound`, which uses log t ≤ t − 1 to get u·log(1 + H/u) ≤ (U + b)·log(1 + X). The e in √e is an error parameter, not Euler's number. `PhiSplit`, the estimate with a c/e term, is defined but never used anywhere in the source. `SlopeIneq` itself comes from `slope_inequality_of_family`, through `function_slope_transfer` and the matrix-lattice estimate `joint_a_ramified_holds`.

   The factor 4 comes before integration, from the frame linear-algebra lemma `final_real1`: w ≤ (η + 4ε + 4√e)·D with ε = u/(u + t) and e ≤ ¼. The integral only turns that profile into a logarithm.
5. **Mean bound**. With the pieces above, the proof gets mean(ASK) ≤ mean(Main) + `errorTermWith`. That error includes m²·La/k, m·(B0 + Nc)·x^(cε), and m·cE·B0.
6. **Size balance** \[item 8, "take m like √X"\]. B0 = 2n·m·b·x^ε, so after normalization one error term shrinks like x/m and one grows like m. They balance at m ≈ √x, giving Gx ≤ x^(½ + (C+3)ε), the error size equivalent to RH. In the construction a\_E = x·b exactly (`aTx`), so the first term is exactly x/m: each counted term is charged x·b against a gain of m·b. With m = x^(½−δ) that term alone is x^(½+δ), so within this error budget fewer lifts cannot certify the RH exponent. Three allowances in errorTermWith feed this x/m scale: divisibility (cDiv·n\_T·a·La), slope (cSlope·n\_T·a·La·(1 + m/k)) and counting (through sumA). The weights themselves cannot shrink: every counted term satisfies |a\_t/b − x| ≤ 1, so sumA lies between n\_T·b·(x − 1) and n\_T·b·(x + 1). Fewer lifts would need the error charged against that weight to be smaller, or an estimate that keeps cancellation before the separate allowances are added. The true error could still be smaller than the budget.

**Status.** All six statements were read in the source. Not yet read: the proofs of the two slope halves (`lower_bound_simplified`, `claimHigh_valid`) and the proof of the mean bound. `SlopeIneq` is used by `Ga_bound5` (in `LevelOfCongruence.lean`), which bounds the weighted divisibility sum Ga from above. The mean step then splits ASK as (ASK − Ga) + Ga through `umean_le_comb`. Only an upper bound on the weighted total of the local differences Σ\_j gain − τ\_w is needed, not a sign at every place. `EComp5` allows an error of order m·B0, but nothing shows it ever gets that large, so the √x balance is between proved upper bounds. Alpöge's 2/3 is probably an earlier version of step 6 in which the width outgrew the gain; the code holds only the final version.

## Worked example: one block opened up (unverified)

A preprint posted 21 Nov 2026 expands Alpöge's footnote 3 into a proof of Deligne's bound for Frobenius eigenvalues over finite fields, the argument in under six pages ([Hexagon 2611.00789v1](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00789v1)). Deligne's theorem itself is long established; this is a proposed shorter route to it.

**The block a reader carries.** Compare two weighted point counts, bound their difference, and use a sign from geometry so a large eigenvalue cannot hide by cancellation.

1. **Same sign.** On a hypersurface Y in k copies of U, set R = QN − M^k (Q the field size, N the count on Y, M the count on U). Under weak Lefschetz only two pieces survive, and Proposition 4 identifies (−1)^(D−1)·R as the trace on their direct sum: one actual vector space. The overall sign drops out when bounding |R|.
2. **Powers bound eigenvalues.** If traces of all powers of an automorphism grow at most like B^n, every eigenvalue has absolute value at most B. With opposite signs this fails: 100 − 99 = 1 can hide two large terms.
3. **Bound, then remove the loss.** Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval bound |R|. Two limits: growth over field extensions bounds the eigenvalues for fixed k, then taking k-th roots as k grows removes the extra loss in the field-size exponent.

**What the six pages add.** They show that one hypersurface gives both the sign and a character sum that can be bounded. Our one-line summary hides that work in the word "suitable".

**Status.** GPT-6 Astra produced the proof from Alpöge's sketch; Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2 checked it, Fable re-proving weak Lefschetz from Katz's article. References: Katz's pages, two statements of Lafforgue and Scholl's introduction were read at the source; SGA 4, SGA 4½ and the Stacks Project were not reopened; remarks on Bombieri, Deligne, Kedlaya and Laumon are from memory. No human has checked the proof or text, and nothing is formalized. No link from this argument to `rhOfAll` has been traced.
